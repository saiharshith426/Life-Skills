# Listening and Active Communication

## 1. Active Listening

# Question 1: What are the steps/strategies to do Active Listening?

1. Give the person my full attention.
2. Don't interrupt while they are speaking.
3. Maintain eye contact.
4. Ask questions if I don't understand.
5. Show that I am listening through small responses or nodding.
6. Repeat or summarize important points.
7. Pay attention to their tone and body language.
8. Don't judge before they finish speaking.


## 2. Reflective Listening

# Question 2: According to Fisher's model, what are the key points of Reflective Listening?

1. Listen carefully and understand the actual message.
2. Pay attention to the person's feelings and body language.
3. Repeat the main point in my own words.
4. Ask questions if something is unclear
5. Confirm that I understood them correctly.
6. Make sure everyone has the same understanding.

For example, after a meeting, I can summarize the important points and ask everyone if I understood them correctly.


## 3. Reflection

## Question 3: What are the obstacles in your listening process?

1. Getting distracted easily.
2. Thinking about my reply while the other person is speaking.
3. Assuming what they are going to say.
4. Losing focus when the topic is difficult.
5. Being tired or stressed.
6. Sometimes hesitating to ask questions.
   

## Question 4: What can you do to improve your listening?

1. Keep distractions away
2. Focus on the person speaking.
3. Take notes during important discussions.
4. Ask questions when I don't understand.
5. Don't interrupt.
6. Summarize important points to make sure I understood correctly.


## 4. Types of Communication

## Question 5: When do you switch to Passive communication?

I become passive when I don't want to argue or make someone uncomfortable. Sometimes I stay quiet even when I have a different opinion.

## Question 6: When do you switch to Aggressive communication?

I can become aggressive when I am angry, frustrated, or when I feel that someone is not listening to me. I may speak harshly instead of calmly explaining the problem.

## Question 7: When do you switch to Passive-Aggressive communication?

I may become passive-aggressive when I am upset but don't say it directly. For example, I might use sarcasm, ignore someone, or give short replies.

## Question 8: How can you make your communication assertive?

- Speak clearly and directly.
- Say what I think without being rude.
- Listen to the other person's point of view.
- Say no when necessary.
- Avoid shouting, sarcasm, and blaming.
- Stay calm during disagreements.
- Focus on solving the problem instead of proving myself right.

For example, instead of saying **“You never explain properly,”** I can say **“I didn't understand this part. Can you explain it again?”**
